\newcommand{\CapLadder}{%
Reachability of a published comparison, as observed on one machine on
\ProbeDate{} by a read-only probe rather than read off a previously recorded
status. Each rung is an assertion that can be checked independently, and the
rungs are ordered by how much they demand. Green marks a rung that holds here and
red marks one that does not. The ladder is monotone, so the first break makes
every rung above it unreachable and the informative quantity is the index of that
break rather than a count of failures. The grey strip records a fact that is
deliberately not a rung: the evaluation corpus is public, openly downloadable and
permanently archived, and is nonetheless absent from this machine, which is a
different situation from either a satisfied or a broken rung.%
}

\newcommand{\CapSweep}{%
Median localisation error against the dispersion of the unknown per-channel
timing offsets, for an offset-agnostic least-squares estimator and an
offset-aware maximum a posteriori estimator, over \NTrials{} paired trials at
each of \NConditions{} conditions. The vertical axis is logarithmic because the
two estimators differ by more than two orders of magnitude at one condition. In
the shaded condition the true offsets are exactly zero, and the ordering between
the estimators reverses: the offset-agnostic estimator recovers the source almost
exactly, while the offset-aware estimator pays a floor cost for estimating
nuisance parameters that are not present. At every condition where offsets do
exist, the offset-aware estimator is the better of the two and its error is
nearly flat, while the offset-agnostic error grows with the dispersion it
ignores.%
}

\newcommand{\CapWins}{%
The same comparison counted per trial rather than summarised by a median. Bars
give the number of the \NTrials{} paired trials at each condition on which the
offset-aware estimator produced the smaller error, and the dashed line marks the
count expected if the two estimators were indistinguishable. Where offsets are
present the offset-aware estimator wins a clear majority but never all trials,
so the advantage is consistent rather than absolute. Where offsets are absent it
wins none at all, which is a stronger and more specific statement than a small
$p$ value: the reversal is unanimous across trials, not an average over a mixed
outcome.%
}
