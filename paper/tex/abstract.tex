Computational reproducibility is often reduced to a binary badge, although
what stops a reader is a particular missing step. We audit one comparison
from the 2024 {IEEE}/{RSJ} International Conference on Intelligent Robots and
Systems ({IROS}) in acoustic sensor calibration with a four-rung ladder: a
citable record, a fixed source revision, an available runtime, and a
recomputable table. A read-only probe found the recorded revision and all
\EntryPoints{} entry points and located the first break at rung \FirstBreak{},
the absent proprietary interpreter the source requires; a free substitute,
also absent, would not satisfy the rung, so the published table is out of
reach. A public corpus of about \CorpusGB{} GB is downloadable yet absent
locally: a resolvable link is not a local asset.

Companion analyses show what a verdict hides.
Across \NConditions{} conditions and \NTrials{} paired trials, the offset-aware
estimator wins at the \NonZeroConditions{} nonzero-offset conditions; at zero
offset its median error is about \ZeroRatio{} times larger and it wins
\ZeroWins{} trials. In a three-level record, mean and median disagree at
\CxDisagreeing{} levels; removing \CxFlipDepth{} trial reconciles them, and the
paired signed-rank test agrees with the median. A sweep over \FineLevels{}
levels, \FineRooms{} arrays, and \FineTrials{} trials places the reversal at
\ReversalLow{}--\ReversalHigh{} ms and leaves the offset-agnostic estimator
ahead at the original nonzero condition in \AnchorAgnosticRooms{} of
\FineRooms{} arrays.

The contribution is an auditable stopping-point and disclosure protocol for one
published comparison, recorded on one machine on one date.
