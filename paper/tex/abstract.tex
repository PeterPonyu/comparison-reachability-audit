Computational reproducibility is often reduced to a binary badge, although a
reader can be stopped by a particular missing step. We audit one comparison
from the 2024 {IEEE}/{RSJ} International Conference on Intelligent Robots and
Systems ({IROS}) in acoustic sensor calibration with a four-rung ladder: a
citable record, a fixed source revision, an available runtime, and a
recomputable table. A read-only probe found the recorded revision and all
\EntryPoints{} entry points, but rung \FirstBreak{} failed because the
proprietary interpreter was absent; free substitutes were absent and are not
equivalent. The top rung is therefore unreachable. A public field
corpus of about \CorpusGB{} GB is downloadable but is not local, so a
resolvable link is not a local asset.

The companion analyses expose what a verdict hides.
Across \NConditions{} conditions and \NTrials{} paired trials, the offset-aware
estimator wins at the \NonZeroConditions{} nonzero-offset conditions, whereas
at zero offset its median error is about \ZeroRatio{} times larger and it wins
\ZeroWins{} trials. In a separate three-level record, mean and median disagree
at \CxDisagreeing{} levels; removing \CxFlipDepth{} trial makes the summaries
agree, while the paired signed-rank test agrees with the median. A
sweep over \FineLevels{} levels, \FineRooms{} arrays, and \FineTrials{} trials
places the reversal at \ReversalLow{}--\ReversalHigh{} ms and leaves the
offset-agnostic estimator ahead at the original nonzero condition in
\AnchorAgnosticRooms{} of \FineRooms{} arrays.

The contribution is an auditable stopping-point and disclosure protocol for one
published comparison, recorded on one machine on one date.
