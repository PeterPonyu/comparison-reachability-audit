%% One falsifiable question, the bounded answer, and the scope it does not leave.

Computational work is usually described as reproducible or not, and artifact
badges reinforce that binary by certifying that code was made available.
Availability is not runnability, and a reader who cannot run an artifact is
rarely told which specific step stopped them. This study proposes recording the
stopping point itself as a checkable object, and applies the instrument to one
published comparison in acoustic sensor calibration. The instrument is a ladder
of \NRungs{} rungs, each an assertion that can be independently verified on a
given machine: that a citable record exists, that the reference source can be
obtained at a fixed revision, that the runtime the source requires is available,
and that the published table can be recomputed. The ladder is monotone, so a
break propagates to every rung above it, and the informative quantity is the
index of the first break rather than a pass or fail verdict. A read-only probe
re-checks every rung against the live machine instead of trusting a previously
recorded status, and its output is what this study reports. On the machine
audited, rungs one and two hold: the reference source is present at a revision
matching its recorded pin, with all \EntryPoints{} of its entry points found.
Rung \FirstBreak{} breaks, because the proprietary interpreter the source
requires is not installed, and a frequently suggested free substitute is also
absent and would not be treated as equivalent if it were. Rung \NRungs{} is
therefore unreachable and no table is synthesised in its place. Corpus
availability is tracked as a separate axis and behaves differently: the
evaluation corpus is publicly archived and openly downloadable at roughly
\CorpusGB{} gigabytes, and is nonetheless absent locally, so a resolvable link
is recorded as distinct from a local asset. The locally reproducible part of the
same project supplies a second, substantive result. A comparison of an
offset-agnostic least-squares estimator against an offset-aware maximum a
posteriori estimator over \NConditions{} conditions and \NTrials{} paired trials
each supports the expected conclusion at every condition where unknown
per-channel offsets are present, by median error and by paired signed-rank test.
At the one condition where no offsets are present, the ordering reverses
completely: the offset-aware estimator is worse by a factor of about
\ZeroRatio{} in median error and wins \ZeroWins{} of \NTrials{} paired trials.
The headline that the offset-aware method is better is therefore true on
\NonZeroConditions{} of \NConditions{} conditions and severely false on the
remaining one, which is the condition a comparison is least likely to report. A
third record shows that reaching the top rung would not have been enough. It
stores both the summary a paper would print and the \CxPlanned{} paired trials
behind it, for two locally written estimators at \CxConditions{} timing-noise
levels. The mean over trials and the median over the same trials name different
estimators at \CxDisagreeing{} of the \CxConditions{} levels, including one at
which every planned trial returned an error in both arms so that no handling of
failures is involved; a paired signed-rank test on those trials agrees with the
median everywhere, and setting aside \CxFlipDepth{} trial in
\CxTrialsPerCondition{} makes the mean agree too. Measured against the
Cram\'er--Rao bound the same trials place both estimators at
\CxEffMeanLow{} to \CxEffMeanHigh{} times the bound or at \CxEffMedianLow{} to
\CxEffMedianHigh{} times it, depending only on the summary. Recomputing a
published table therefore certifies a number and not a claim, so disclosure of
the per-trial record is recorded as a second axis beside corpus availability;
here it would have cost \CxBytesMinimal{} bytes, less than the summary it
accompanies. A fourth result shows that disclosure is not sufficient either.
The offset comparison places its reversal at a single condition because its grid
steps from zero to \CoarseGapHigh{} milliseconds of dispersion with nothing in
between; rerunning the same estimator code on \FineLevels{} dispersions inside
that interval, crossed with \FineRooms{} independently simulated microphone
arrays at \FineTrials{} paired trials per cell and \FinePairs{} trials in total,
finds the offset-agnostic estimator ahead on median error out to between
\ReversalLow{} and \ReversalHigh{} milliseconds depending on the array, and
still ahead at the original grid's lowest non-zero condition in
\AnchorAgnosticRooms{} of \FineRooms{} arrays. The sign a sweep reports is thus
a joint property of the estimators and the grid, and the conditions a design
skipped leave no trace in the trials it kept, so no amount of per-trial
disclosure recovers them; one additional condition does, and its position was
predictable from the original record's own printed medians to within
\PredictionError{} milliseconds. No claim of novelty in acoustic localisation is
made, no estimator compared here is anyone's released code, no substitute
runtime is treated as equivalent to the one required, the refined sweep is a new
run reported as an extension rather than as a pre-registered test, and the audit
describes one machine on one date rather than a permanent property of the work
examined.
